THE PROBLEM • MANDATE & LIMITS OF VIDEO AI

Why Public Transit Fails Deaf Travelers — And Why Video AI Can't Fix It.

The legal and operational breakdown at 15,000 U.S. airport boarding gates, and why generative video models fail in spatial physics.

TRANSIT PROBLEM • 14 CFR § 382.53(a)(3)

Airport Information is Built for Hearing Passengers

When a gate assignment changes or an urgent boarding announcement is made, it is broadcast via overhead audio. Deaf travelers can miss flights when announcements are acoustic.

Under federal regulations (14 CFR § 382.53(a)(3)), carriers and airport authorities that control gates share responsibility to provide passengers who identify as needing hearing assistance with prompt access to the same flight information.

Stationing certified human interpreters 24/7 across 15,000 domestic gates is financially and logistically impossible ($150+/hour with 48-hour advance booking windows). Automated visual infrastructure is the only scalable path.

Mandate: Prompt access to the same flight information at all commercial gates
TECHNOLOGY FLAW • BLACK-BOX 2D GENERATION

Why Black-Box Generative Video Fails

Big Tech generative video models (diffusion, video LLMs) generate unconstrained 2D pixel grids. Because they lack an underlying anatomical skeleton, they frequently hallucinate: spawning extra knuckles, melting fingers together, and stretching bones.

In sign language, spatial precision is grammar. A 2 cm drift in hand location inverts the meaning of a sign (e.g., FATHER on the forehead vs. MOTHER on the chin). Hallucinated pixels produce linguistic gibberish.

Furthermore, streaming generative video to thousands of airport gate displays costs $0.15–$0.30 per query and incurs 15–30 second generation latencies. SAMMY eliminates server GPU streaming entirely by rendering locally in browser WebGL.

SAMMY Solution: Invariant bone lengths (Δ = 0.000) • Sub-100ms response
