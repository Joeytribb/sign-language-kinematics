01 // Linguistics 101

What is ASL? Sign Language is Not English

ASL is a standalone, natural visual-spatial language with its own morphology, syntax, and discrete phonology.

In 1960, linguist William Stokoe proved that sign languages are not crude pantomime or finger-spelled English. Every sign is formed by combining 5 discrete phonological parameters (analogous to vowels and consonants in speech):
1. Handshape
(DEZ)

Specific finger & thumb configurations (fist, open palm, index point, bent V). Over 30+ distinctive shapes.

2. Palm Orientation
(SIG)

Direction the palm faces (up, down, inward, outward) and pointing vector of extended knuckles.

3. Location
(TAB)

Anatomical contact loci relative to the body (forehead, chin, chest, neutral signing space).

4. Movement
(MOTION)

Trajectory path (straight, arc, wavy, rotational, contact tap) and repetition cadence.

5. Facial NMMs
(GRAMMAR)

Eyebrows, mouth morphemes, head tilts. Over 50% of ASL grammar lives on the face!

William Stokoe (1960) • Battison's Phonological Laws (Symmetry & Dominance) Key insight: Shifting a finger position by just 1.5 cm creates a completely different word (Minimal Pair)!
02 // The Computational Data Layer

How Sign Language Becomes Code: ASL-LEX & HamNoSys

We don't guess animations. We compile discrete phonological data structures into continuous 3D physics.

HamNoSys (Hamburg Notation System)
The "IPA" & AST of Sign Language

Developed at the University of Hamburg, HamNoSys is a machine-readable phonetic transcription system. It breaks any sign into an exact geometric vector:

Sign_Token = { Handshape: 0x4B, Palm_Rot: (0°, 90°, -45°), Locus: "CHIN_CONTACT", Motion: "ARC_FORWARD_20CM" }

To a software engineer, HamNoSys is an Abstract Syntax Tree (AST). It gives us a discrete token format that our C++ compiler turns into continuous skeletal joint rotations!

ASL-LEX (The Master Lexical Database)
2,700+ Quantified Linguistic Vectors

Created by researchers at Boston University, Tufts, and San Diego State, ASL-LEX is the world’s gold-standard quantitative database of American Sign Language.

  • Catalogs thousands of signs with 200+ phonological parameters.
  • Encodes exact frequency, sign duration, iconicity, and grammatical class.
  • Acts as our ground-truth validation dictionary to verify that our 3D avatar's hand configurations match biological reality.
Sehyr et al. (ASL-LEX, 2021) • Hanke et al. (HamNoSys, University of Hamburg) We treat HamNoSys & ASL-LEX as the "Bytecode" our kinematic engine compiles.
